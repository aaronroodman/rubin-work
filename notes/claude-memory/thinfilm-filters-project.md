Multilayer interference-filter design study for future Rubin narrow/medium-band filters. Lives in `rubin-work/filters/`.

**Engine of record: tmm_fast** (PyTorch, `pip install tmm_fast`) — validated against Steven Byrnes' `tmm` to <1e-8 on a 15-pair QWOT reflector. Key advantage: PyTorch **autograd on layer thicknesses** → gradient-based design. API gotcha: N shape `[S,L,W]` (stacks,layers,wavelengths), T shape `[S,L]` in **meters**, and N/T/lambda/Theta must all be the same type (all torch tensors or all numpy).

**tmmax is BROKEN in the installed build** (0.x): multilayer coherent cascade returns wrong results (15-pair QWOT reflector gives R~0.31 instead of ~1.0; single layers OK). Do not use for multilayers. Its **nk_data/ dispersion tables are good though** (refractiveindex.info; SiO2, Ta2O5, Nb2O5, TiO2, HfO2, MgF2… at `site-packages/tmmax/nk_data/numpy/*.npy`, rows = [wl_µm, n, k]). `thinfilm.py` loads n from there, k optional.

**Files:** `thinfilm.py` (materials + tmm_fast wrapper), `design_gband.py` (autograd design driver), `validate_engines.py`.

**v1 (design_gband.py):** freestanding 44-layer/3.2µm lossless demo, in-band ⟨T⟩=0.98 — proof the shape is reachable.

**v2 (design_gband_v2.py, filterstack.py) — realistic:** 80-layer bandpass (SiO2/Ta2O5, graduated longpass+2 shortpass QWOT blockers, Adam with OD-aware log-barrier loss) + 4-layer backside AR (⟨R⟩=0.08%) on fused-silica substrate, k(Ta2O5)=2e-4. Incoherent substrate combine T=T1·a·T2/(1−R1'R2'a²). Results: normal-incidence peak 0.986, in-band ⟨T⟩=0.896 (≥ Rubin g's 0.887/0.928), out-of-band OD>3 over 355-390 & 564-790nm. Edges steeper than real filter (10-90% blue edge 3nm vs Rubin's 20nm).

**Beam model (works!):** Rubin annular f/1.23 cone = incidence angles **14-23° in air** (marginal arcsin(1/2.468)=24°, ~0.6 obscuration → 14° inner). Étendue weight w(θ)∝sinθcosθ, unpolarized (mean s,p). Effect on 150nm-wide g band: edges blue-shift ~7-10nm + soften (10-90% 3→7nm), in-band ⟨T⟩ 0.896→0.872. Small for broadband — will be severe for narrow bands (next step). `filterstack.beam_average(theta_lo=14,theta_hi=23)`.

**Gotchas:** tmm_fast needs n+ik (positive imag=absorption); n−ik triggers a "gain" assertion. tmmax database k is bogus-high (SiO2~0.003) — use `thinfilm.K_REAL` constants instead. Substrate index forced k=0 (bulk loss via sub_internal, negligible for fused silica).

**Narrow/medium band (design_narrow.py):** 30nm band @475nm needs a Fabry-Perot cavity (edge-filter stopbands are ~120nm wide, too broad). 3-cavity FP [(HL)^2 2H (LH)^2]×3 (29 layers, 2.3µm) + AR, optimized to flat ~29nm top. **Rubin beam result:** the band sweeps 11nm across the 14-23° cone (475nm@14°→464nm@23°); beam-averaged center shifts −7.4nm (−1.6%), peak 0.989→0.959, edges smear (blue 10-90% rise ~3→12nm), FWHM ~preserved (~29nm), integrated throughput only −2%. Takeaway: medium bands usable but must be designed ~7nm red of target rest-λ to compensate; the 11nm cone-sweep would wash out true narrowbands (≤10nm) → that's the feasibility limit. FP cavity-count tradeoff: 1-cav=Lorentzian, 2-cav widened=double-hump, 3-cav=flat top (use 3+).

**Two-surface OD4 filter (design_twosurface.py, plot_twosurface.py):** Rubin-style split — LWP (longpass) on FRONT face blocks blue, SWP (shortpass) on BACK face blocks red; combined incoherently through substrate via filterstack.system_T (front=LWP, ar-slot=SWP). Key: split does NOT reduce OD depth per side (blue OD all from front, red OD all from back) but avoids coherent ripple between the two big stacks + eases fab. High index switched to **Nb2O5** (n2.3, dispersion table to 2.5µm → physical NIR; Ta2O5 table stops at 887nm). Front=1 QWOT stack@344nm×26pair (52 layers, OD>4.9 blue); back=3 graduated shortpass stacks @615/775/1015nm ×17pair (102 layers) for continuous red block. Total 14µm. **Result: OD4 across full Si range 320-1080nm — normal worst-OD 4.13, beam worst-OD 3.90** (only 2 pts @717nm grazing, the 615/775 overlap nudged by cone); peak 0.930 (≥Rubin 0.928), in-band ⟨T⟩ 0.857 normal/0.817 beam. Gotchas: (a) beam blue-shifts stopband EDGES → design red coverage to ~1160nm so cone-shifted edge still clears; leaks appear at graduated-stack overlaps + coverage top-edge; (b) blocking only needed to Si cutoff (~1080nm, Si gap 1107nm) — don't chase >1080. Metric must exclude ±10nm passband-edge transitions (not real leaks).

**Narrow 25nm@410 + OD4 blocking (design_narrowblock.py):** front=3-cav FP defines 26nm band, back=incoherent wide blocker (LWP@393 + 4 graded SWP to 1120) passes ~35nm window + blocks rest. Band works (collimated 26nm@411 peak 0.92); **beam degrades it hard** — center −5.6nm→405, peak→0.87, edges smear badly (11nm cone-sweep = 44% of 25nm width). **OD4 blocking NOT robustly achievable in the beam:** normal ~OD3.4 worst, **beam collapses to ~OD2.3** (deep-red 800-1080 & blue-far), even at 19µm/211 layers (>practical ~10µm limit; more layers = diminishing returns). **Root cause: I optimized the blocker at θ=0 only; dielectric stopbands are angle-dependent, so OD4@0° leaks across 14-23°.** Key lessons: (1) blocking MUST be optimized over the beam angles (angle-averaged OD loss) — clear next step, recovers part of gap; (2) all-dielectric OD4 narrowband blocking in f/1.23 is at/beyond practical limit → real route is **absorptive/colored-glass blockers (angle-independent)** for deep rejection + dielectric for the narrow passband. filterstack.system_T reused with front=cavity, ar-slot=big blocker.

**Beam-optimized blocker (design_narrowblock_beam.py) — RESOLVED the beam-blocking problem:** re-optimized the back blocker so the loss drives the étendue-weighted beam-averaged T (5 angles 14-23°, s+p) below OD4 instead of θ=0. Optimizer needed lr=0.3 + grad-clip(1.0) + keep-best (Adam diverged at lr=1.0 with stiff log-barrier — pass window collapsed). Also up-weight pass (w_in=30, block region has ~30× more points) and start red block at 445nm (24nm guard from 421 pass edge; cavity covers 421-445). **Result: beam-opt blocker holds OD4 across essentially all out-of-band 450-1080nm under the cone (mostly OD5-6, one 9nm spot at 562-571 OD3.7), band preserved (beam peak 0.92, center 412→406, FWHM 27nm).** vs normal-opt blocker which leaked at ~10 spots (OD2-3). **Only remaining walls = near-band shoulders 388-395 & 425-435nm (OD~1.3-3)**: within ~15-25nm of a 25nm band the ~10nm cone-smear exceeds the passband↔stopband guard — fundamental for dielectric in a fast beam. → those shoulders need an angle-independent **absorptive/colored-glass blocker**, or a wider guard/wider band. Files: narrowblock_beam.npz, figs/narrowblock_beam.png.

**Hα/[NII] 10nm @655nm (design_halpha.py):** same architecture (3-cav FP m=3 = 41 layers giving 9.5nm@655 + beam-opt back blocker), but band in MIDDLE of Si range so back must block BOTH wide blue (322-632, 3 graded longpass) AND red (668-1080, 2 graded shortpass) = 154 layers, 17µm total. Blocker gotcha: keep stopband edges clear of the pass window (blue top stopband ends 634, red bottom starts 671; window 636-664) or the blocker fights the band. **VERDICT: a 10nm band is NOT viable at f/1.23.** The cone shifts it **−9.6nm (655→645)** — nearly its own width — so a filter designed for Hα operates at 645 and MISSES the 654.8/656.3/658.4nm lines; peak 0.885→0.789, FWHM 9.5→10.9nm, band sweeps 650(14°)→641(23°) so effective center varies ~9nm across the focal plane. Blocking: beam-opt holds OD4 across most of 320-1080 but the 28nm pass window (barely > ~13nm beam smear) degrades to beam-pass 0.6, and isolated leaks remain (363,463,628,872-895,1060-1080nm, OD~3-4). **Practical floor for Rubin ≈ 25-30nm medium bands; true ≤10nm line filters need a slow/collimated beam Rubin lacks at the filter.** To place a 10nm band on a line you'd design ~10nm red + accept ~20% peak loss + field-dependent center. Fig figs/halpha_design.png.

**Hα corrected (scan_halpha.py, design_halpha_v2.py):** pre-shift design to the red so the beam lands on Hα. **Key finesse-scan result — the beam FWHM FLOORS at ~9.4nm (the cone smear); going thinner in the collimated cavity does NOT narrow the operational band, only kills throughput:** m=2 coll24nm→beam25nm T(Hα)0.89; **m=3 coll9.5nm→beam10nm T(Hα)0.81 (sweet spot)**; m=4 coll3.8→beam9.4 T0.32; m=5 coll1.6→beam9.4 T0.10; m=6 coll0.8→beam9.3 T0.03. So thinnest USEFUL Hα band ≈ m=3 3-cav FP designed at 666nm → beam-centered on Hα (656.4nm), FWHM ~11nm, **T(Hα)=0.79**, captures full Hα+[NII] 654.8-658.4 complex. 41+154 layers, 17µm, beam-opt blocker OD4 across most of 320-1080 (isolated leaks ~OD2.7-3.7 at overlaps/near-band). Figs halpha_v2.png, design_halpha_v2.py; scan_halpha.py has the finesse table. Design rule: pre-shift center by +~10nm (mean cone shift at 655) to land beam on target line.

**Higher-index materials study (compare_materials.py, figs/material_comparison.png):** transparent high-index options at 656nm (k≈0): TiO2 n2.40, ZnSe n2.53, CdS 2.37, vs Nb2O5 2.31, Ta2O5 2.09 (Ta2O5 has k6.5e-3; ZnS k0.03 too lossy; Si/Ge absorb — db Si k is wrong). Two effects, both confirmed: (1) **higher contrast → sharper edges** — collimated FWHM at fixed m=3 drops 15.7(Ta2O5)→9.4(Nb2O5)→7.8(TiO2)→5.8nm(ZnSe); (2) **higher effective index → smaller cone-smear floor** — the operational beam-FWHM floor drops only modestly 10.7→9.3→9.0→8.3nm (roughly ∝1/n*², sublinear). Throughput maximized when collimated FWHM≈floor (so tune m per material; at fixed m=3 Nb2O5 matches best, T@Hα0.86). **Verdict: higher index gives genuinely sharper edges / fewer layers, but only shaves the Rubin operational floor ~9.3→8.3nm — does NOT break the ~8-9nm f/1.23 cone floor.** Practical pick: **TiO2** (durable, standard visible narrowband, n2.40, k0). ZnSe lowest floor but soft/hygroscopic (IR material) = physics bound. Lowering nL (MgF2 1.42 / porous silica ~1.2) further sharpens edges but doesn't help the floor (floor set by high-index spacer's n*). To go substantially narrower needs reducing the beam angular range at the filter, not a coating change.

Next options: (a) absorptive blocker for near-band shoulders; (b) center-λ × bandwidth **degradation map**; (c) redder bands (smaller fractional cone shift). Relates to [[optatmo-moments-project]] (also JAX/optical).
